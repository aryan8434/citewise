Botric system design

How botric.ai is put together: the marketing site, the product app, the API, the chat widget and the jobs behind them. [1]System map

Botric is five public front doors on one API. The marketing site and the app are separate Next.js projects; the chat widget is a separate React bundle that customers load on their own sites.

What every page shares

  • Header: an announcement bar, then AI Visibility, Content Agent, AI Support, Track ▾, Explore ▾, Pricing, Contact, Sign in and a Start 7-Day Free Trial button.
  • Body: sections numbered 00–09 in a side rail, and a closing "Run Free Audit" block (a GET form to /geo-optimizer?url=…).

The GEO layer: how the site gets itself cited

Crawler access

robots.txt explicitly allows GPTBot, ChatGPT-User, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, Claude-Web, anthropic-ai, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, Bingbot and Meta-ExternalAgent.

Files written for LLMs

/llms.txt (with the score weights and a page map), /llms-full.txt, /ai/faq.json, /ai/service.json and /.well-known/ai.txt.

Structured data everywhere

JSON-LD on all 73 pages. FAQPage appears on 55 of them, because FAQ answers are what engines lift most easily.

Content pipeline

Posts are MDX files in Git with frontmatter, deployed on push. Botric says its non-technical content team publishes using Claude Code instead of a CMS.

Onboarding

Nothing is saved until the last step, so a user who leaves at the plan screen leaves no half-built workspace behind.

  1. Website: workspace name, URL and one target country. In the background Botric fetches the favicon, pulls a colour palette from it, and drafts a business profile.
  2. Brand: review the drafted brand name, summary, up to 5 core topics (ordered; the first is primary) and brand colours.
  3. Competitors: Botric searches each topic and pre-selects up to 5, spread across topics. The user can add, remove or retry.
  4. Pages: Botric crawls internal links; the user picks the pages the first Site Audit covers.
  5. Plan: new accounts must pick Free or start a 7-day paid trial here. Existing subscribers skip this step.
  6. Creation: the workspace and site are saved, the tracked prompt set is generated from the topics, and Site Audit and AI Visibility start in parallel. The user lands on Get Started.

[6]GEO run pipeline

AI Visibility is the core report. A run passes through ten stages, which the app names in its progress banner. The order below is the manual's.

Every prompt ends in one of four outcomes

OutcomeMeaningWhat it becomes
Named and citedBrand in the answer, and a brand URL among the sourcesNo gap
Named, not citedThe engine knows the brand but quoted someone elseContent gap: publish a page worth citing
Cited, not namedA brand page is a source, but the brand isn't namedPositioning gap: state the brand and claim on that page
Not mentionedNo name, no URLInvisible for this prompt

Score, engines and prompts

GEO score

Mention rate 40% + citation rate 25% + position 20% + sentiment 15%, computed per platform and overall. Bands: 90+ excellent, 80–89 good, 70–79 fair, below 70 needs improvement.

Engines

Marketing covers ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode. The manual also lists Copilot, Grok, DeepSeek, Meta AI, Mistral and Poe.

Prompt intents

Informational, Commercial, Comparison, Buyer intent, Branded, Locational, Adjacent, Direct and General. Marketing groups them as direct, comparison, category and informational.

Prompt Research

A test bench: up to 10 prompts a run at 20 credits each, answered on every platform. The prompts worth keeping get promoted into add-on slots.

The report has five tabs: Overview (brand perception, score history, competitor heatmap, top cited sources), Prompts, Content Gaps (ranked Critical to Low), Recommendations (content gaps, entity and schema work, "publish the fact", pitch a story, claim a listing) and Actions (high priority, long-term and no action, each markable as Fixed). Citations sorts sources into owned, competitor-owned, editorial, community, docs, publisher and other, with YouTube and Reddit broken out. Competitors adds a benchmark, a list of every brand named, and a gap matrix of prompts against rivals.

[8]Content engine

The Content Agent is two tools on one context layer. The Blog Agent writes new pages from gaps; the Page Agent rewrites pages the audit already graded.

  1. Build the Content Brain from up to four signals: Site Audit (required), AI Visibility, SERP intelligence (run on demand) and Search Console (optional). The Brain is organised into three views: My Business, My Market and My Progress. The user sets the author name and tone. A build takes a few minutes and shows live progress.
  2. Discover topics (20 credits a run). Each idea is scored for citation likelihood and shows where it came from: a gap, a competitor advantage or a Search Console query. Users can add their own topics.
  3. Generate a blog (200 credits). Status moves Generating → Ready; a failure shows "Failed · refunded".
  4. Score the draft on citability, readability, originality and humanization. Export as Markdown, HTML, Word or PDF.
  5. Publish and record the URL, which marks it Completed. Then check AI crawlability in Site Audit and re-run AI Visibility to see whether the prompt moved to "named and cited".

Page Agent lists every audited page by score and issue count. For one page it generates concrete fixes: rewritten titles and meta descriptions, heading corrections, missing sections, claims to state outright, and structured-data and entity additions. It needs only a completed Site Audit, not a Brain.

Marketing describes more: a twelve-dimension Google and LLM "rankability" score and a 15-point GEO checklist (named author, JSON-LD, Speakable schema, 6-question FAQ, comparison table, external citations, answer-first openers and more). The manual documents only the four scores above.

[9]Support agent

Engine 02 is a chat agent trained on the workspace's knowledge sources and embedded with one script tag. The request paths below come from the widget's own code.

Knowledge

  • Website URLs, pasted text, FAQ pairs, files (PDF, DOCX, PPTX, TXT, MD), audio and video (transcribed; higher plans)
  • Each item is chunked, embedded and tracked through training
  • Sources live on the workspace; each agent picks which to read
  • FAQs are generated 30–60 s after training

Agent settings

  • Six tabs: Info & Appearance, Knowledge, Channels, FAQs, Connect, Settings
  • Three modes: floating widget, always-open iframe, or a modal opened by the site's own button
  • Every colour configurable; branding removable on higher plans
  • Allowed-domains list; a disable switch

Channels

  • Ticket system, lead form (text, email, phone, number, text area), support-form intake, web search fallback, Cal.com booking, exit-intent popup
  • Planned: WhatsApp, Slack, Telegram

Conversations and analytics

  • Logs tagged Support, Knowledge Gap or Unclear; tickets (Open → Closed, with priority); leads; calls
  • Each answer is marked confident, tentative or unanswered
  • Chat is billed per 200 messages; history kept 60, 90 or 120 days by plan

[12]Scale, security and operations

Load

An Infinity customer (by the pricing page) is 150 prompts × 5 engines = 750 captured answers per site per week, and up to 3,750 across 5 sites. Each answer then gets several LLM passes (names, citations, tone). Answer capture is the scaling bottleneck and the main cost.

Async by default

Runs, audits, Brain builds and blog generation are all background jobs. Users can leave the page; progress arrives over SSE and sidebar badges update when a job finishes.

Tenancy

The organization is the billing and access boundary. Workspace ids sit in every app URL. Members see all workspaces; roles only gate rename, delete and billing.

Widget safety

A public key (bt-live-…, which suggests live and test keys), an allowed-domains list per agent, and Shadow DOM isolation. The iframe route is public by design.

Google data

GA4 and Search Console are connected by OAuth with read-only scopes under Google's Limited Use policy (privacy policy, section 7).

Retention

Chat history is kept 60, 90 or 120 days by plan. A TTL index or a nightly purge job would enforce it Inferred.

[13]What doesn't add up

Places where Botric's marketing, docs and product disagree. Worth knowing before you copy any of it, and worth avoiding in your own build.

  • Prompt limitsThe pricing page promises 15 / 25 / 150 prompts on Bit / Byte / Infinity. The manual says the tracked caps are 10 / 20 / 25 per site, plus a fixed number of add-on slots.
  • Score formulaThe FAQ and llms.txt say mention 40, citation 25, position 20, sentiment 15. The brand-mentions tracker page lists mentions, citations, content coverage and sentiment instead.
  • Engine countThe homepage says 4 assistants, the trackers say 6 engines, the manual lists 11 platforms, and the tracker FAQ calls Grok and DeepSeek "on the roadmap" while the manual lists them.
  • Blogs on InfinityThe plan card says 40 blogs; the note under the comparison table says 15.
  • Blog scoringMarketing describes 12 rankability dimensions and a 15-point GEO checklist; the manual documents 4 scores.
  • GEO billingA run bills the full tracked cap plus add-ons regardless of visible prompts. The manual itself calls it "the one that surprises people".
  • GEO courseThe hub promises 18 modules; 8 are live, and module 9 returns 404.

Copy this, avoid that

Worth copying

  • Credit ledger with pre-flight blocking and automatic refunds
  • The four-outcome vocabulary for every prompt
  • Per-engine scores next to the overall score
  • Prompt Research before a prompt takes a tracked slot
  • Running your own product on your own marketing site

Do differently

  • Bill only for the prompts actually run
  • One source of truth for plan limits, used by the pricing page and the app
  • Official APIs first; label results "via API"
  • Don't promise course modules or engines that aren't live
